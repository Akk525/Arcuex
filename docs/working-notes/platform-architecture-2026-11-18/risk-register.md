# Arcuex platform transition risk register

Date: 2026-11-18. These are proposed engineering/governance controls, not a legal opinion, an actuarial assessment or certification. Owners are roles to assign before the relevant work starts, not existing appointments. All ten risks are open. Severity reflects potential impact, not a quantified probability estimate.

## Evidence and interpretation

The primary process document is `Autonomous_AI_Insurance_Quote_Bind_and_Claims_Approach.docx` in the original Arcuex root. Sections 1/11 distinguish a research-backed process proposal from approved wording/authority, section 5 rejects a universal AI risk score, section 6.1 distinguishes coverage triggers, section 7.1 covers preservation/confidentiality, and section 8 supplies the nine-checkpoint adjudication framework. Its cited insurer examples are not adopted as law or carrier-approved operating rules here.

For general governance context, the [IAIS Insurance Core Principles and ComFrame](https://www.iais.org/activities-topics/standard-setting/icps-and-comframe/) provide an insurance-supervision framework. Their existence does not determine Arcuex's license, a partner's authority or requirements in a chosen jurisdiction. The [IAIS fair-treatment application paper announcement](https://www.iais.org/2025/07/iais-publishes-application-paper-on-fair-treatment-of-a-wide-range-of-consumers/) describes attention to customers across design, distribution, claims and complaints. These are reasons to preserve product conduct and post-claim controls in the architecture, not a compliance checklist or certification. Both official pages were checked 2026-11-18.

The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/) is voluntary governance guidance. Its official page, checked 2026-11-18, says the framework is being revised. The plan uses risk governance, documented evaluation and human/AI responsibility boundaries as design considerations; it does not claim conformance or substitute this framework for insurer approval or applicable legal requirements.

No regulator, insurer or actuary was contacted in this assignment. No market, legal jurisdiction, risk carrier, pricing model or AI provider was selected. Those decisions remain explicit release dependencies for real-world operation; local synthetic schema and dry-run tests can proceed after engineering-plan approval.

## Required decisions before expanding scope

1. Name the initial software customer and contractual operator: insurer/MGA software, authorized partner arrangement or another reviewed risk-bearing structure. Identify who may publish, quote, bind, adjudicate and authorize settlement.
2. Agree the first real product's intended insured, territories, trigger/notice mechanism, wording, expenses/sublimits, benefit method, complaints/appeals, capacity and supported rail/payment timing. Do not assume the enterprise-AI source template applies unchanged.
3. Approve organization/tenant and authority-root boundaries before persistent multi-organization services; decide whether the beta remains explicitly single tenant during migration.
4. Agree publication/version/digest compatibility and what historical mappings can honestly prove. Preserve legacy policies rather than upgrading their meaning retroactively.
5. Define measurable evidence, adversarial-test and oversight criteria for advisory examination before any proposal to delegate deterministic decisions. AI-provider integration and automated authorization require distinct later scope decisions.

These decisions do not authorize production migration, new financial transactions, a claim on the demonstrated policy, external publication or generic platform implementation.
